# StayTilted: brand discovery report

Prepared 27 October 2026. Desk research only: no interviews, no analytics, no paid competitor tools. Every figure below is tagged with its source; anything I could not verify is listed under "Gaps".

**Brief as given:** photography studio, South Africa, offering weddings, portraits, events and brand work, but leading with a niche for cosplayers.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- The cosplay audience is reachable in person: South Africa's two big conventions are large, annual and growing. Comic Con Africa (Johannesburg) draws about 70,000 people a year; Comic Con Cape Town drew 34,000 in 2026 and expects about 40,000 in 2027.
- Cosplayers' loudest complaint about photographers is behavioural, not technical: silent photographers, no direction, poor lighting, unclear image use, missing credit. A studio that fixes those has something concrete to promise.
- I found no South African photographer who positions on cosplay. That is a research result, not proof none exist (see Gaps). If it holds, the lane is open.
- The status-quo competitor is probably "get a friend or a convention walk-around photographer to do it", not another studio. I could not verify this.
- Conventions are also a business audience: Comic Con Africa 2026 had 303 exhibitors, which is a route for the events and brand work.
- The site currently leads with weddings ("the first dance, mid-laugh"). That contradicts the stated focus and should change.
- Next dated opportunity: rAge, 27 to 29 November 2026, Fourways Mall, Johannesburg, cosplay included. That is about a month away.

## 2. Audience

Only one segment can be supported from the research. The others are hypotheses to test.

**Segment A: the working cosplayer (evidence-backed in part).**
- Behaviour: makes or commissions costumes, competes or performs at conventions, wants photographs that show the build and the character. Comic Con Africa's programme includes daily casual competitions and a Championship of Cosplay; Cape Town's championships have four categories including Needlework and Armor, so craft is judged and worth documenting.
- Jobs to be done (inferred from the sources below, not interviewed):
  - Functional: get sharp, well-lit, well-framed pictures of a costume that took weeks to make.
  - Emotional: feel safe and respected during the shoot.
  - Social: have images worth posting and crediting.
- Frustrations (from a single US-based article, The Mary Sue, so treat as a hypothesis for South Africa): photographers who don't direct poses, repeat the same pose in bad light, charge a lot for weak results, post without credit or crop watermarks, are vague about where images will be used, and in some cases behave predatorily. Cosplayers vet photographers themselves, which takes effort.
- Cosplay Commons (Comic Con Africa coverage) notes photographers often ask for repeat poses and says "the best convention photos begin with mutual respect".

**Segment B: the cosplay-adjacent business (hypothesis).** Exhibitors, artists and event organisers at conventions. Comic Con Africa 2026 had 303 exhibitors.

**Segment C: the existing general audience (hypothesis).** Couples, families and portrait clients. Not researched; the brief says they remain services.

## 3. Competitors

### Direct: cosplay-focused photographers in South Africa
**None identified.** My searches returned international cosplay photographers (UK, US, Philippines) and general South African portrait and event photographers, but no South African photographer or studio presenting cosplay as their specialty. Search engines under-index Instagram, where most of this work lives, so this needs a manual check (see Gaps). Do not treat it as a confirmed empty field.

### Indirect
- **General portrait and event photographers.** Indicative Johannesburg prices from one blog (getphoto.co.za, no year stated): mini portrait R800 to R1,500; couples R1,200 to R2,500; event photography R1,500 to R3,500 per hour; wedding R5,000 to R35,000+. Useful as a ceiling for what a client compares you to, not as verified market rates.
- **Convention-provided photo services.** Comic Con Africa 2026 reportedly had a photography stand and a photo-op zone with celebrity guests (mybroadband). These are guest photo-ops, not cosplayer shoots.
- **International cosplay photographers** reachable online.

### Status quo (unverified, hypothesis)
Friends or fellow cosplayers photographing each other, phone photos, and convention walk-around photographers. The Mary Sue piece describes the last as oversaturated at US conventions; I found nothing equivalent for South Africa.

## 4. Category and problem space

**The problem.** A cosplayer has invested time and skill in a costume and wants it photographed well, by someone they trust, in conditions that suit the build.

**Structure.** Convention-led. The calendar sets demand:

| Event | Venue | Dates | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Comic Con Africa 2026 (happened) | Johannesburg Expo Centre, Nasrec | 24 to 27 Sept 2026 | gauteng.net, EWN |
| rAge 2026 | Fourways Mall, Johannesburg | 27 to 29 Nov 2026 | tech.africa |
| Comic Con Cape Town 2027 | CTICC (larger hall, CTICC 1) | Dates not verified | Wikipedia |

Attendance figures (as reported):
- Comic Con Africa: over 47,000 in 2018; about 71,000 in 2019; about 80,000 in 2022; about 70,000 in 2024 and 2025 (mybroadband).
- Comic Con Cape Town: 27,484 in 2023; over 32,000 in 2024; 34,000 in 2026; about 40,000 expected in 2027 (Wikipedia).

These are total attendees, not cosplayer counts. I could not find a cosplayer headcount.

**Conventions in the category (what every photographer does the same way).** Photograph at the convention itself, in the hall, in daylight or flat exhibition lighting. Conventions are therefore the convention to consider breaking, or serving better.

**Shifts.**
- rAge became a broader festival format for 2026 (esports, creators, anime, cosplay).
- Cape Town is moving to a bigger hall.
- Comic Con Africa's cosplay page lists an international guest line-up and a championship, but says nothing about photography.

**Vocabulary.** Cosplayers talk about builds, needlework, armor, characters and "transformation" (Cosplay Commons, Comic Con Cape Town page). Wedding-style language ("capture your moment") would read as foreign.

**Moats.** Reputation inside the community and trust; convention access. Neither is held by a clear incumbent in my research.

## 5. Positioning territories (candidates, not decisions)

**1. The shoot is directed by the character.**
- Statement: a photographer who works with the cosplayer on poses, framing and light for the specific character.
- Why it could work: it answers the top documented complaint (silent, non-collaborative photographers).
- Who: competition-level and aspiring cosplayers.
- Competition: none found locally.
- Risk: easy to claim, needs a portfolio and testimonials to prove. We have neither yet.
- Rejects: fast walk-around snaps and "stand there and smile".

**2. Shot like the scene it's from.**
- Statement: tilted, cinematic framing that treats each cosplay like a still from its own story.
- Why it could work: the name already means this. A tilted frame is a known film and comic device (my general knowledge, not from the research).
- Who: cosplayers who want striking, shareable images.
- Competition: unknown.
- Risk: a tilted-frame look can read as a gimmick if overused. It also needs real photos to show.
- Rejects: flat, centred, documentary convention photos.

**3. Trust and consent first.**
- Statement: clear credit, honest image use, proper watermarks and releases, and a photographer who respects boundaries.
- Why it could work: directly answers the complaints about credit, usage and predatory behaviour.
- Who: cosplayers, and especially parents of younger cosplayers (hypothesis).
- Competition: nobody found making this their headline.
- Risk: trust claims are only credible with proof. Do not publish them until you have genuine policies and real testimonials.
- Rejects: unclear usage terms, uncredited reposts.

**4. More time than the convention floor allows.**
- Statement: planned shoots at locations or in the studio, around the convention rather than at it.
- Why it could work: it removes the rushed, crowded conditions that cause weak convention photos.
- Who: cosplayers with a finished costume and a clear shot list.
- Competition: unknown.
- Risk: asks the client to pay and travel separately from the convention day.
- Rejects: shooting only in the hall.

**5. Cosplay first, same eye for everything else.**
- Statement: the cosplay niche leads; weddings, portraits, events and brand work follow from the same approach. This includes themed or cosplay weddings (hypothesis) and photography for convention exhibitors.
- Why it could work: it fits the brief you gave and the 303-exhibitor figure shows a business audience.
- Who: cosplayers first, then adjacent businesses.
- Competition: general photographers can't claim the cosplay credibility; cosplay specialists (if any) may not do weddings or events.
- Risk: diluting the focus. A site that says "everything" reads as generic.
- Rejects: the current wedding-first presentation.

## 6. Implications for the site

- **Lead with cosplay.** The hero, first gallery and first service should be cosplay. Weddings, portraits, events and brand work stay as later sections.
- **Replace the first-dance placeholder content.** The captions ("The first dance, mid-laugh") and the "Weddings and couples" order are wedding-first.
- **Add what the research says matters:** a short "how a shoot works" section and plain terms on credit and image use. Only publish commitments you will keep.
- **Use real cosplay photos** as soon as you have them. Placeholders do not prove anything here.
- **Link bookings to the calendar** (rAge in November; Cape Town and Comic Con Africa dates once announced).
- Keep the tilt: it is your distinctive device, and territory 2 builds on it.

## 7. Gaps and open questions

Things this research could not establish:

1. **Direct competitors.** I found no South African cosplay photographers, but search engines do not index Instagram or TikTok well. Someone needs to search those platforms by hand (hashtags such as the local convention tags, plus "cosplay photographer" with each major city) and list who is active, their prices and their style.
2. **Prices for cosplay shoots in South Africa.** Not found. The only local price source is a general Johannesburg blog with no year.
3. **Cosplayer numbers.** Convention attendance is not the same as cosplayers. I found no count of South African cosplayers.
4. **Interviews.** None done. The photographer complaints come from one US article, so they may not match what South African cosplayers say. Five to eight short interviews with local cosplayers would test territories 1 to 4 directly.
5. **Competitor traffic and SEO.** No Similarweb or Ahrefs access in this session, so there is no traffic or search-overlap data.
6. **Dates.** Comic Con Cape Town 2027 and Comic Con Africa 2027 dates were not verified.
7. **Your own data.** Past enquiries, client feedback, Instagram insights and any existing portfolio were not available. They would sharpen everything above.
8. **Source dating.** The LensCulture project on South African cosplayers describes a cosplay-at-home series and is old; I used it only as colour, not as evidence.
